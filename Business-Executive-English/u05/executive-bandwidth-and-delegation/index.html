Executive Bandwidth & Delegation

When every urgent task lands on your desk, saying yes to everything stalls the whole team. Decide what stays with you, what gets handed off, and how to delegate with clarity.

Intelligibility Focus: Calm Executive Stress

When leaders delegate in a rush, flat intonation sounds stressed or abrupt. Stress the action word and leave a brief beat before the deadline.

RUSHED / FLAT
"Can you just run with it and loop me in on Friday."
CALM EXECUTIVE STRESS
"Go ahead and RUN with it — just LOOP me in before FRIday."

04 · Receptive Notice

Listening: Clearing the Bottleneck

Listen to Daniel, an Operations Manager, checking in with his mentor Claire. Keep the transcript closed during your first listen.

1-on-1 Coaching Call · Daniel & Claire
Duration: ~55 seconds · Focus: Identifying bottlenecks and handing off ownership
Claire: Daniel, you look stretched thin today. How is your operational bandwidth looking this week?
Daniel: Honestly, I'm maxed out. I spent three hours this morning formatting vendor updates and checking every slide for Thursday's review.
Claire: That sounds like a classic bottleneck. Why not have Sofia handle the weekly vendor updates? She already knows the accounts.
Daniel: I thought about that, I just worry something might slip if I take a hands-off approach.
Claire: You don't have to disappear. Let her run with it, and ask her to loop you in if a deadline changes. That will immediately take three hours off your plate.
Daniel: Fair point. What about the Q3 budget pack?
Claire: Get the final numbers signed off by finance tomorrow, and empower your two team leads to present their own sections on Thursday. Keep the big risks on your radar, not the font sizes.

05 · Grammar in Context

Causative & Delegation Verbs

Instead of saying you will do every task yourself, executives use causative and delegation structures to assign clear ownership while focusing on results.

Structure Affirmative Negative Questions (Yes/No & Wh-)
have + person + base verb
Assign a task directly (no "to")		 I'll have Sofia handle the vendor call. We shouldn't have senior leads format slides. Did you have Leo check the figures?
Who can we have lead the kickoff?
get + thing + past participle
Focus on the finished outcome		 Let's get the budget signed off by Thursday. We didn't get the memo approved in time. Can we get the draft reviewed today?
When will we get this resolved?
empower / trust + person + to + verb
Grant real autonomy (uses "to")		 I want to empower the team to make that call. I don't want to ask her to work late again. Do you trust your lead to run the QBR?
What do you need me to take over?

06 · Live Communication

Delegation Triage Game

It is Monday morning and you only have room for TWO major priorities. Click each card to mark it as Keep, Delegate, or Pause—then defend your plan out loud.

Speaking Checklist (What Great Looks Like)

✓ Clear decision: State which 2 tasks you keep and why they require your direct presence.
✓ Accurate delegation: Use at least two target structures (have [person] + verb, get [thing] + past participle, or empower [person] to + verb).
✓ Executive chunks: Include at least two expressions naturally (take off my plate, run with it, loop me in, on my radar).
✓ Calm delivery: Use clear stress and brief pauses instead of rushing through your plan.
