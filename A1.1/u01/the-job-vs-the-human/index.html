05 · Understand & Practice

Indefinite Articles (a / an) + To Be for Jobs

In English, always use a or an before a singular job. Never use a / an with plural jobs!

Teacher Sequence · Activity 5A: Guided Discovery (a vs. an vs. Plural) Strict Scope [N]
Límite estricto [F]: Prohibido introducir rutinas en Present Simple (works, teaches, drives) ni What do you do? Enfócate 100% en To Be + a/an + job.
1. Regla visual simpleSeñala las 3 tarjetas superiores: a + sonido consonante (a teacher), an + sonido vocal a, e, i, o, u (an artist), Ø en plural (We're teachers).
2. Interferencia L1 (Español)El error #1 en hispanohablantes es omitir el artículo ("I'm engineer") o ponerlo en plural ("We are a teachers"). Contrasta ambos en voz alta.
a + consonant
a teacher · a nurse
a driver · a chef
an + vowel
an engineer · an artist
an architect
Ø + plural
We're teachers.
They're engineers. (No a/an!)
Form Singular (a / an) Plural (No a / an)
Affirmative (+) I'm a nurse. / He's an engineer. We're nurses. / They're engineers.
Negative (−) I'm not a chef. / She isn't an architect. We aren't chefs. / They aren't artists.
Yes/No Question (?) Are you a driver? → Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Is she an artist? → Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.		 Are they teachers?
Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Speaking Checklist

1. Use a or an accurately before singular jobs (I'm a nurse / I'm an engineer).
2. Correct at least one wrong guess (No, I'm not a chef!).
3. Ask your partner at least 2 questions with Are you a/an...?
4. Use at least one Everyday Expression (It pays the bills / Just for now / That's awesome! / Good for you!).
